Bank of England

Bank Rate rises from 3.75% to 4%

Meeting ended: 4 November 2026 · Published: 5 November 2026 · Next decision due: 17 December 2026

  • The Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) voted 8–1 to raise Bank Rate by 0.25 percentage points. One member preferred a larger increase to 4.25%.
  • The Bank said the conflict in the Middle East disrupted energy supplies and transport, raising fuel costs and household utility bills. The economy was also stronger than expected, increasing the risk that higher energy costs would feed into other prices.
  • UK CPI inflation was 3.5% in September, above the Bank’s 2% target. In its November decision, the Bank projected inflation could reach around 5% by mid-2027. It had seen little evidence so far of significant effects on other prices or wages, but raised rates to help limit that risk.

As of 26 November 2026, the Bank’s rate page lists current inflation at 3.6%, against its 2% target.

Sources: November 2026 Monetary Policy Summary and Minutes · Bank of England: latest Bank Rate decision

What it can mean for borrowers

  • Tracker mortgages: if your deal directly tracks Bank Rate, your mortgage rate will typically rise by 0.25 percentage points. Check your lender’s notice for when your new payment takes effect.
  • Other variable-rate mortgages: lenders set their own standard variable rates, so the timing and size of any change can vary.
  • Fixed-rate mortgages: your rate does not change during your fixed term as a result of this decision. Rates on new fixed deals may move before or after an announcement because lenders also price in market expectations and funding costs.
  • Remortgaging: if your deal is ending soon, compare the total cost of new offers, including fees and any early repayment charge if you switch before your deal ends.

What to watch next

The next MPC decision is due on 17 December 2026. The Bank is watching energy prices, inflation and wage growth. This increase does not commit it to raising rates again.
